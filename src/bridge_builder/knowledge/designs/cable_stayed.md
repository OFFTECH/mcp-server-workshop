## How it carries load

Pylons stand on the inner anchors (x=-6 and x=6) and rise to y=9. Stays run
diagonally from the pylon down to the deck and hold it from above. Beams only
join neighbouring nodes, so every stay is a chain of 2.83 m beams; a chain in
tension behaves like a cable. The stays pull the pylon towards the river, so
backstays from the same pylon heights down to the bank anchors pull it back.
The stays are parallel (a "harp" layout), and the longest pair meets at mid-span.

## Key geometry

Left half (the right half is its mirror image, x -> -x):

- Pylon: (-6,3) -> (-6,5) -> (-6,7) -> (-6,9).
- Stays: (-6,9) -> (-4,7) -> (-2,5) -> (0,3); (-6,7) -> (-4,5) -> (-2,3); (-6,5) -> (-4,3).
- Backstays: (-6,9) -> (-8,7) -> (-10,5) -> (-12,3); (-6,7) -> (-8,5) -> (-10,3); (-6,5) -> (-8,3).
- Deck: y=3 from x=-6 to x=6.
- Grow each stay and backstay from the pylon outwards.

## When to choose it

The most beams and the highest cost of the reference designs, but it shows how to
use the banks: the backstays anchor the structure outside the gap. The pylon base
collects every stay's pull and is the most loaded beam. Without the backstays the
pylons lean in and the bridge fails.
