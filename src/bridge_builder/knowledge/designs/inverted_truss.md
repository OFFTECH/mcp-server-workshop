## How it carries load

The same triangulation as the overhead truss, mirrored below the road. The bottom
chord at y=1 hangs in tension, the deck works in compression, and the two end
diagonals carry the load from the bottom chord back up into the anchors.
Nothing sticks up above the road.

## Key geometry

- Deck: y=3 from x=-6 to x=6.
- Hangers: x=-4, -2, 0, 2, 4, from y=3 down to y=1.
- Bottom chord: y=1 from x=-4 to x=4.
- End diagonals: (-6,3)->(-4,1) and (6,3)->(4,1).
- Inner diagonals: (-4,3)->(-2,1), (-2,3)->(0,1), (0,1)->(2,3), (2,1)->(4,3),
  symmetric, pointing down towards mid-span.
- Below the deck, nodes exist only inside the gap (x=-4..4 at y=1 and y=-1);
  the bank walls at x=+-6 are solid.

## When to choose it

When the space above the road must stay clear, and when money matters: it ties
with the overhead truss as the cheapest reference design and has by far the lowest
peak load. It hangs below the road, though, so nothing tall can pass under the
bridge. Built entirely of wood it still crosses (2520). The inner diagonals make it a truss:
without them the deck hangs from the bottom chord like a chain from a cable, sags,
and comes within a whisker of snapping.
